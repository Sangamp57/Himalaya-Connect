Why we are here

A journal for
the curious.

Himalaya Connect began with a simple frustration: too much travel writing tells you what a place looks like, and too little tells you what it feels like to pay attention there.

Our point of view

Specific, not sweeping.
Curious, not certain.

The Himalaya is not one story. It is thousands of neighbourhoods, kitchens, workshops, languages and weather systems. We resist the tidy summary.

Our writers begin with a detail: a carved window, a bowl of noodles, the way a bus fills up before dawn. From there, they follow the thread into history, craft, politics or memory — always with a named place and a real person in view.

We are independent, free to read, and edited for accuracy. We correct mistakes openly. We do not accept payment for coverage.

Editorial principles

Three things we hold onto

01

Rooted in place

We name the neighbourhood, the maker and the season. We do not use a photograph of one valley to stand in for a whole country.

02

Edited with care

Stories are read for accuracy, context and respect before publication. When we get something wrong, we say so and change it.

03

Open to everyone

No paywall, no sponsored detours. Members can save stories, respond and publish their own field notes.

Travel with care

Leave room for
ordinary life.

01

Ask first. A photograph is a relationship, not a souvenir. When someone says no, that is the answer.

02

Pay fairly. Use local guides, drivers, cooks and craftspeople. Ask what a fair price is rather than bargaining to the lowest number.

03

Do not perform the place. Dress and behave with the same consideration you would in a friend's home. Sacred sites are not content sets.

04

Stay curious. Learn the name of the valley, the fruit, the festival. Let the place be more complicated than your itinerary.
